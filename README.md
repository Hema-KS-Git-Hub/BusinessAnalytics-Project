# BusinessAnalytics-Project

				                             Healthcare Business Domain
Data Sources

		Encounters: Records of patient visits, including dates, types, and associated costs.
		Patients: Demographic and identifying information for individuals receiving care.
		Procedures: Details of medical services performed during encounters.
		Payers: Insurance providers or entities responsible for covering healthcare costs.
		Organizations: Healthcare facilities or networks delivering patient services.

Tech Stack

		SQL — Data extraction, transformation, and aggregation.
		Tableau — Interactive dashboards and visual analytics.
		Excel/CSV — Initial data profiling and quick checks.


SQL:
						Cost-Conscious Care: Risk Analysis in the Patient Journey 

	This project focuses on uncovering the financial risks and burdens faced by patients during their healthcare journey, with a 			particular emphasis on costs not covered by payers.
 
						Key Highlights:
		 
	Designed a relational data model with linked tables to ensure data integrity and consistent relationships between 						encounters, patients, payers, and procedures.

	Combined multi-source healthcare datasets to create a unified view of patient encounters and associated costs.

	Applied targeted filters to identify high-cost cases, such as encounters exceeding $10,000, enabling focused cost-control 				analysis.

	Evaluated payer contributions and cost coverage to reveal patterns in financial exposure for patients.

	Analyzed encounter durations to identify extended stays and their potential impact on overall treatment costs.
 
TABLEAU:
						Risk and Revenue: A Strategic Analysis of Patient Encounter Costs 
		 
	This project provides an interactive visual analysis of encounter costs across different care settings, highlighting both 				financial risk areas and opportunities for operational improvement.

						Key Highlights:

	Compared costs across encounter types — including ambulatory, emergency, inpatient, and outpatient — to identify high-cost segments.

	Identified recurring high-cost encounters to uncover patterns that may indicate chronic conditions, resource-intensive treatments, or 	inefficiencies.

	Tracked procedure cost trends over time to detect emerging cost drivers and correlated them with outcome-related factors.

	Mapped geographic variations in patient traffic and average encounter costs to support region-specific planning and resource 			allocation.
